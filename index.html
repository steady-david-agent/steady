          A moment before eating · 1 min 44 sec

          Read transcript

          Before you eat, try taking one small pause. It doesn't have to be long, and it doesn't have to happen perfectly. You might notice that you're hungry, that you're reaching for food out of habit, or that you've already started eating. Whatever you notice is useful information, not a grade.

          If you're hungry, you can eat. There is no need to wait to earn your meal. Take a moment to consider what sounds satisfying and what is available to you. Then, if it feels practical, give the first few bites your attention. Notice the temperature, the texture, and the flavor. You don't have to analyze every bite; just notice one or two things.

          Partway through, you can check in again. How does your body feel now? Are you enjoying the food? Would you like more? You are allowed to keep eating, and you are allowed to stop. The aim is to hear your own signals more clearly, not to follow a rule about exactly when to finish.

          And if you notice that you ate quickly or while distracted, that is not a failure. It is simply a moment you noticed. You can take the next bite with a little more attention, or you can carry that awareness into another meal.

          Every small pause is practice. With time, these ordinary moments can help you build a steadier, kinder relationship with eating, one choice at a time.

          To try this in real life, pick a meal or snack you already expect to have. Before the first bite, take one comfortable breath. Notice one sensation in your body and one thing about the food in front of you. Then eat. Later, if you remember, check in again. There is nothing to make up if you forget; the next chance to notice will come along.

          Returning to the practice · 1 min 47 sec

          Read transcript

          Building a habit doesn't mean getting every day right. It means finding a way back when life gets busy, plans change, or you simply forget. That return is not a backup plan. It is part of the practice itself.

          Think of one moment when you might open Steady today. Perhaps it's around a usual meal, or when you notice yourself reaching for a snack. When you check in, choose the answer that fits: hungry, an urge, or already ate. You don't have to change your answer to make it look better. An honest check-in gives you a place to begin.

          If you miss that moment, check in when you remember. A missing entry means only that no entry was recorded. It doesn't tell the story of your day, and it doesn't erase the effort you've already made.

          You can also look back with curiosity. Was there a time when you felt rushed? Did a meal feel especially satisfying? Is there one small thing you'd like to notice next time? You don't need a dramatic reset. A simple intention is enough: maybe you'll sit down for the first few bites, or pause to notice how you feel.

          Here is the encouragement: progress is built from many ordinary returns, not from a flawless streak. You are learning a skill, and learning takes repetition. Be honest about what happened, treat yourself with respect, and take the next helpful step. You can begin again at the very next opportunity.

          For a practical start, choose one reminder time that fits your day and add it in Steady. When the prompt appears while the app is open, give an honest answer. If you reopen it later that day, you can answer then. If the day gets away from you, let tomorrow be another opportunity. You are not starting from zero; every time you return, you are practicing a skill you already know.

          A Kind Reset in the Middle of a Meal · 1 min 44 sec

          Read transcript

          Sometimes you notice you're eating on autopilot only after you've already started. Maybe you're standing in the kitchen, working at your desk, or reaching into a bag while thinking about something else. That moment of noticing is not too late. It's the beginning of the practice.

          Try this the next time it happens. Put down whatever you're holding for a moment, if that feels comfortable. Let your shoulders soften. Take one ordinary breath. You don't need to stop eating, explain yourself, or make up for anything. Just ask: What am I noticing right now?

          Perhaps the food tastes good. Perhaps you're hungry, distracted, tired, or looking for a little comfort. More than one thing can be true. See if you can notice the taste and texture of the next bite, and whether sitting down or taking a short break from your screen would make the experience feel more intentional. Then decide what you need. You can continue eating, pause, or change your surroundings. There isn't a single correct answer.

          Here's the chizuk: progress isn't a perfect streak. Each time you notice and choose how to respond, you're practicing a skill that can grow. A difficult moment doesn't erase the care you've already shown yourself, and it doesn't have to dictate the rest of your day.

          If you'd like, use Steady to record a simple check-in. Keep it factual: “I noticed I was distracted, and I took a breath.” That's enough. Tomorrow brings another chance to practice, but you don't have to wait until tomorrow. Your next moment is available now.
